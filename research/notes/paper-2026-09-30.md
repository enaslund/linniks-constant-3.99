# The 3.99 paper and the verification pass that produced it (2026-09-30)

The graded-near proof at `L=3.99` is now written up as a self-contained paper,
[`paper/linnik399.tex`](../../paper/linnik399.tex) (source in `paper/sections/`,
bibliography `paper/references.bib`, 148 references in the initial version). A flattened single-file
version with the bibliography inlined is in `paper/dist/` (see
[paper/README.md](../../paper/README.md)). The paper contains no code: the flattened
file compiles with `-no-shell-escape`, and a scan finds no `\write18`, `\directlua`,
`filecontents`, `\special`, `minted` or similar constructs.

**Status.** The paper states the theorem `P(a,q) ≪ q^{3.99}` and gives a complete
proof modulo the published inputs it quotes (Heath-Brown 1990 and 1992,
Xylouris's dissertation, Burgess, Graham, Jutila) and the finite computations it
describes. All reviews so far are by AI agents; **no human expert has refereed
it.** The formal-verification scope is unchanged (see STATE.md). The repository
is private, so the data-availability URL in §14 is not yet reachable by readers.

*[Added 2026-10-02: the certificates, programs, reports and Lean sources are now public in this repository; see [computations/README.md](../../computations/README.md). The paper's §14 cites it.]*

## How the paper was checked

Writing the paper was used as a new verification pass over PROOF.md and the code.
The reviews are preserved in [reviews/2026-09-30-paper](../reviews/2026-09-30-paper/README.md):

* five component deep dives (notation and costs, near lemma, location, case tree,
  certificates), each rederiving its part from the printed sources and the code;
* four referee reports on the draft (A: §§1–5 and 8; B: §§9–11; C: §§6, 7, 11–13;
  D: numbers, attributions and the literature), and a final consistency pass (E).

None found a blocking error. Every major item had a fix of a few lines, and none
changed a certificate number or margin.

## Gaps found and closed in the paper

These are gaps in the written argument (PROOF.md or the draft), not in the
certificates. The paper is now the most complete statement of the proof; where
it and PROOF.md differ, the paper is correct.

1. **The 1/108 conductor saving** had rested on an unproved remark of
   Heath-Brown (§16, item 4). The paper proves the weighted form it needs
   (§7.4: Lemmas `Lbounds` and `explicitphi`, Proposition `conductor`). This used
   a `4·rad q` bound for real primitive moduli.
2. **The old first-family bound `J_old`** was asserted without proof when `p<b`
   and when `λ₁>p`. Proposition `firstinside` now covers both cases.
3. **Order of constants (quantifiers).** The paper now fixes:
   * a finite anchor set `𝒜` (grid plus all leaf anchors `a`, `p*`, `p°`);
   * the disc radius `δ₀`, fixed after the tolerances;
   * an explicit budget for the smoothing parameter `θ`;
   * a separation `M` chosen with `η''_min`;
   * `T*(q)` as the least admissible value.
4. **Heights in the "no zero to the right" lemma.** The two-test and paired
   rows use it at heights up to `2` and `l+1`. It is now stated for `|γ|≤2l`.
5. **The paired first-family proposition** was stated for the exact `(m,D_*)`,
   but the code checks the rounded pair `(m',D')`. It is now stated and proved
   for any `m'≤m`, `D'≥D_*`. Kept-zero multiplicities are handled explicitly,
   using (X3.4) rather than the disc form.
6. **The λ′ rows (Prop. `polyrows`).** Their certified supremum is over
   `y ≥ p_row − a`, where `p_row` is the parent row's bound from X Table 2′.
   This prior bound was not stated before. It holds on every cell by Prop.
   `parentrows`. Referee C checked that each `old_lower` equals the parent's
   `p_j`, and that sampling with `y` down to 0 leaves the margins unchanged.
   *Suggested hardening, not done:* assert `lp ≥ old_lower` in
   `endgame.reduced_spec`.
7. **Sub-case configuration sets** (rc height split, second-zero split) are
   now defined. `ρ'` is fixed as a zero of `χ₁`, not of `χ̄₁`, so that `y` is
   well defined.
8. **Detection proposition** is now stated for a single `q` (it was stated
   "for all large q", which the regimes do not give).
9. **Parent rows.** A row `(t,t)` gives its bound on the whole parent
   interval, using the trivial bound beyond `t`. The case split is now written
   out; it covers complex `[.82,1.5]` with `p=.827`.
10. **X (4.34) side condition** is now verified with interval arithmetic,
    `computations/core/code/x434_check.py`
    (`results/x434_condition.json`). The supremum over `λ₁∈[.44,.80]`,
    `λ₂∈[.44,1.18]` and all heights is `≤0.0031`, against `5f(0)/48=0.1351`.
    It had been sampled only (about 0.0026). The (4.29) result file
    `results/alias_condition.json` (0.003343) is now committed as well.
11. **The disc radius and its tolerance** (referee E). `δ₀` is now fixed for the
    tolerance `H₀ε₁/8` that Lemma `anchored` uses; it had been fixed for
    `H₀ε₁/4`, the last open link in the quantifier chain.
12. **HB Table 4, row 0.3.** Heath-Brown's side conditions (8.6) and (8.8) hold
    for every row from 0.35 on, but not for row 0.3 on all of `λ₁≤0.3`. That
    row (`λ′≥2.293`) follows instead from HB Table 3 (`λ₁≤0.30 ⇒ λ′≥2.43`),
    checked against the printed table. The parent rows are unchanged.
13. **The fixed-term reservation.** It made the second-family equality
    infeasible as written; the LP count is now `n_E`, which is 0 without
    columns. Referee E's corpus scan found every reserved leaf using columns,
    so no certificate is affected.
14. Smaller corrections:
    * the theorem's range in the tiny-zero case;
    * HB Lemma 8.4, which had been quoted with its parts merged;
    * the `σ>1` range in `Lbounds`;
    * HB Table 4's side conditions (8.6) and (8.8), which hold; the tightest
      slack is about 2·10⁻⁶;
    * `R` may be enlarged in HB Lemma 13.2, so that Lemma 13.3 applies with
      `λ≤m₂`;
    * Cor. `ordinarycost` and the representatives are now stated for every
      character, as Theorem `large` uses them;
    * the rounding directions of the leaf data are listed in full.

## Known caveats (recorded in the paper)

* **Unformalized parts.** The following are proved by hand and checked by
  computer, but are not formalized in Lean:
  * the case tree and zero location;
  * the per-character costs;
  * the exterior regimes;
  * the two-test row (37 roots);
  * the count-type integers.
* **Sieve heights.** The graded rows' heights are regenerated in binary64 and
  are not stored. This does not affect soundness, but an exact replay of the
  stored certificates assumes the same `libm` rounding.
* **Effectivity.** `q₀` and `C` are expected to be effective but have not been
  computed.

## Editorial revision

On September 30, the manuscript received a full editorial revision at the author's
request. The main source and all sixteen section files were revised, and both PDFs
and the standalone submission file were rebuilt. The resulting manuscript has
67 pages and cites 122 references; the bibliography database is unchanged.

The introduction now leads with the result, explains the three kinds of information
about zeros used in the proof, and guides the reader through the case analysis.
The literature discussion is shorter. Definitions distinguish character families,
zero occurrences, representatives, and additional zeros. Every theorem-style
statement has a descriptive title. Hypotheses, parameter ranges, and quantifier
dependence are stated more explicitly, especially in the near-density estimates,
location results, and passage from zeros to a leaf program. The text distinguishes
unscaled analytic quantities from stored integer coefficients throughout.

Several corrections to the exposition deserve an explicit record:

* Restricting the strip used to select a representative can increase its
  normalized distance `λ` from the line `σ=1`; geometrically this moves the zero
  left, not right. The far-density discussion now has the correct direction.
* Disc sums in the explicit formula need not consist only of zeros in the
  rectangle `R(l)`. Their scope is distinguished from that of the finite
  zero configuration.
* Selection of the second and third families removes previously selected
  character families once, avoiding an ambiguous product quotient that could
  divide by a real-character factor twice.
* The first-family analytic contribution has a separate symbol from the stored
  integer coefficient `first`. The realization proof subtracts `final/S`,
  rather than the integer `final`, from an unscaled objective.
* The zero form of the graded lemma now states its required tolerance `η''`
  and the range of its auxiliary parameters in the hypotheses. Uniformity and
  permissible rounding directions are explained where they are used.
* The large-zero exterior uses one near row together with the far-density
  budget. Its concluding description now mentions both ingredients.
* The final adjustment of the constant `C` concerns finitely many reduced
  residue classes for small moduli. Effectivity is distinguished from the
  existence assertion actually established in the manuscript.

The parent-location table now lists all 58 implications explicitly, in two
panels, with the meaning of each row in the caption. The presentation also
separates analytic coverage, interval enclosures, exact certificate checks,
floating-point audits, and the conditional scope of the Lean results.

**Restored at the author's request.** The revision had removed three things that the author had
asked for, and they were restored within its new introduction:
* the author asterisk, and the first-person statement on the use of AI after the abstract. It
  now also records the Codex edit, and the duplicate subsection the revision had added to
  Section 14 was removed;
* the comparison table with Heath-Brown and Xylouris, with its styling;
* the Heath-Brown material:
  * the quotation from §16, item 9, with the context of his (11.4) and (11.5);
  * the passage on where the new lemma replaces his counts `N(λ)`;
  * his remark on rounding errors;
  * the values 0.9943 and 0.998, and Xylouris's estimate of 4.96.

The abstract again names the new lemma as the weighted form of Heath-Brown's Lemma 12.1.

The revision's shorter literature survey had left 26 references uncited. They were cited again:
* in the introduction, the originators of the three principles, the simpler proofs of Linnik's
  theorem and the books, explicit and uniform results for progressions, the exceptional-zero
  literature, and special moduli;
* in Section 2, the background texts;
* in Section 12, the Siegel, Tatuzawa and Pintz references and the explicit Deuring-Heilbronn
  paper.

The manuscript now has 69 pages and cites all 148 references.

## Terminology audit

The author asked whether "leaves", "nodes" and the other terms of the case analysis are defined
before they are used. A scan of every section listed the first use of about 70 terms and where
each is defined. Some case-analysis terms appeared before their definitions:
* "node", "refinement tree", "case tree", "location update" and "exclusion";
* "representative", used in §5 and defined in §6;
* "entry", "feature", "diagonal", "correlation term", "family term" and "detector", used in §§2, 5
  and 6 and defined in §8;
* the row kinds, used in §9.2 and listed in §9.5;
* "certificate tree", "terminal box" and "relaxation case";
* "first family" and "second family", which were never named where they are defined.

Changes made:
* The vocabulary in §2.4 has three new items: representatives, the case tree (nodes, location
  updates, exclusions, leaves, subcases) and the far budget. The near-row item was widened to
  cover entries, features, diagonals, the correlation term, family terms, detector, Gram test
  and the row kinds, and the certificate item to cover certificate trees, terminal boxes,
  relaxation cases and certificate exclusions.
* Definition 2.1 names the first, second and third families and the first zero.
* Section 7 marks "parent rows" as a definition, and Section 11 marks "base cells" and "gap cases".
* The census table now follows §11.4, after the terms it uses.
* Pointers were added in §§2, 5 and 9.
* The introduction explains what a certificate tree is.

After these changes a rescan finds no term used outside the introduction before its
definition. The introduction introduces its terms informally, with forward pointers. The paper
has 80 pages.
